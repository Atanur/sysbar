Your Mac, at a glance.

Sysbar is a tiny, native system monitor for the menu bar. CPU, GPU, memory, power, disk and network in a few pixels, temperatures and fan control, keep awake with the lid closed — and your Linux servers and other Macs over SSH, when you want them.

Small on the outside. Thorough on the inside.

Everything a menu bar monitor should do, and nothing it should not.

No jitter

Fixed-width slots mean the menu bar never shifts between 3% and 100%. Pick exactly the metrics you want.

History that follows the rate

Every card keeps a graph of its last 150 samples: 15 seconds at the fastest rate, 5 minutes at the default, 12.5 at the slowest. Cards stay black, white and grey until a load is worth noticing. Click one to open Activity Monitor.

QuietModerateHighCritical

Power that makes sense

Charge and time left on battery. Plugged in, a bolt beside the charge: green while it charges, plain once it is full or held. What goes into the battery is drawn below what the Mac uses.

Both directions at once

Disk, network and power draw two lines either side of a zero line on one scale: read and write, download and upload. Disk shows the room left, network whether it is Wi-Fi or Ethernet.

Temperature and fans

A panel names what is warming the Mac — processor or graphics work, cooling down, or macOS slowing down to cool — and a thermometer joins the menu bar only when the heat is out of the ordinary. Set the fans by hand with one slider.

Keep awake, lid closed

For an hour, until a time, while charging, or for as long as an app stays open — it ends on its own and says why. It can nudge the mouse while you are away, and on a Mac mini it simply keeps it from sleeping.

Top apps

CPU, memory, GPU and energy per app, helpers folded into their app. CPU is a share of the whole Mac, so the rows add up to the CPU card.

Updates in place

A new release shows up in the popover. Sysbar installs it only if it is signed by the same developer, then relaunches.

Private by design

No account, no analytics. Besides the SSH sessions you set up, Sysbar only asks GitHub once a day whether a new release is out.

Your servers, when you ask.

Add a Linux VPS or another Mac and pick it from the top of the popover. Sysbar sends a small read-only script over your existing SSH setup and shows CPU, memory, load, network, disk and GPU.

  • Nothing to install on the server.
  • Keeps watching for as long as you choose after you close it — off, minutes, or always — so the graphs are there when you come back.
  • Your ssh keys or ssh-agent. Never passwords, never auto-accepted host keys.
  • A failed login is not retried, so it will not trip fail2ban.
  • Its own refresh rate, five seconds by default, to keep the server quiet.

What's new

Every release, in plain words. Sysbar offers each one in the popover as it comes out.

0.5.4 Oct 27, 2026

  • What's new, in the app: Settings › About shows the changes in the version you run, and the update banner's What's new unfolds the notes of the new release before you install it.
  • A changelog for every release, on the website and in CHANGELOG.md.
  • The website and the README describe everything Sysbar does today, from temperature and fans to keep awake.

0.5.3 Oct 27, 2026

  • The Power card's corner shows a bolt whenever the Mac runs on its adapter: green while the battery charges, plain once it is full or macOS holds the charge. A Mac without a battery always shows the plain bolt.

0.5.2 Oct 27, 2026

  • CPU in the Top apps table is now a share of the whole Mac, like the CPU card above it, so the apps no longer seem to add up to more than the total. Shares under 10% keep one decimal.
  • Remote devices divide their processes' CPU over the server's cores in the same way.
Earlier releases

0.5.1 Oct 26, 2026

  • Lighter on the CPU: the kind of network connection is looked up once per connection instead of on every sample.
  • The fan icon no longer spins. It is drawn still, and turns amber, orange and red as the fans work harder.
  • App memory switches to gigabytes from 1000 MB, so "1023M" reads "1.0G".

0.5.0 Oct 26, 2026

  • A calmer look: cards are black, white and grey, and colour appears only where it means something, as load turns amber, orange and red, and charge turns green.
  • Disk, Network and Power draw both directions either side of a zero line: read and write, download and upload, and what the Mac uses next to what goes into the battery.
  • The Disk card shows the room left on the disk, the Network card whether you are on Wi-Fi or Ethernet, and the GPU card its temperature.
  • Right-click the menu bar item for Settings, Check for updates, About and Quit.

0.4.0 Oct 26, 2026

  • Keep awake can move the mouse when the Mac is idle, so chat apps do not show you as away. About once a minute the cursor takes a short curved trip and comes back. Nothing is clicked or typed, and it needs the Accessibility permission, which Sysbar asks for only when you turn it on.

0.3.3 Oct 26, 2026

  • Keep awake works on Macs without a lid (Mac mini, Studio, iMac): it keeps them from sleeping while idle.
  • A new MacBook icon for keep awake, struck through while the Mac is kept awake.
  • The popover closes when you click outside it.

0.3.2 Oct 24, 2026

  • Keep awake has a menu with two new choices: until a time of day, and while the charger is connected.
  • "While an app is open" no longer ends when the app briefly disappears, and keep awake records why it ended by itself.

Install

One command with Homebrew. Sysbar needs macOS 14 or later on Apple silicon. It is not on the App Store because it reads power data through a system interface the App Store does not allow.

# Homebrew
$ brew install --cask atanur/sysbar/sysbar
 
# or build from source
$ git clone https://github.com/atanur/sysbar.git
$ cd sysbar && ./scripts/bundle.sh
$ open build/Sysbar.app
