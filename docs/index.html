Small on the outside. Thorough on the inside.

Everything a menu bar monitor should do, and nothing it should not.

No jitter

Fixed-width slots mean the menu bar never shifts between 3% and 100%. Pick exactly the metrics you want.

History that follows the rate

Every card keeps a graph tinted by load. It always holds 150 samples, so it spans 15 seconds at the fastest rate and 12.5 minutes at the slowest.

LowModerateHighCritical

Power that makes sense

Charge and time left on battery; adapter and wall draw when plugged in. Estimates are labelled as estimates.

Top apps

CPU, memory, GPU and energy per app, with helper processes folded into the app they belong to.

One click deeper

Click any card to open the matching tab in Activity Monitor, or Battery settings for power.

Private by design

No account, no analytics. The only connections Sysbar makes are the SSH sessions you set up.

Your servers, when you ask.

Add a Linux VPS or another Mac and pick it from the top of the popover. Sysbar sends a small read-only script over your existing SSH setup and shows CPU, memory, load, network, disk and GPU.

  • Nothing to install on the server.
  • Keeps watching for as long as you choose after you close it — off, minutes, or always — so the graphs are there when you come back.
  • Your ssh keys or ssh-agent. Never passwords, never auto-accepted host keys.
  • A failed login is not retried, so it will not trip fail2ban.
  • Its own refresh rate, five seconds by default, to keep the server quiet.

Install

One command with Homebrew. Sysbar needs macOS 14 or later on Apple silicon. It is not on the App Store because it reads power data through a system interface the App Store does not allow.

# Homebrew
$ brew install --cask atanur/tap/sysbar
 
# or build from source
$ git clone https://github.com/atanur/sysbar.git
$ cd sysbar && ./scripts/bundle.sh
$ open build/Sysbar.app
