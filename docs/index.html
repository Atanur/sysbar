Where it lives

In the menu bar, or around the notch.

Sysbar starts in the menu bar. Move it around the notch whenever you like: Settings › General › Location.

Around the notch

The readings sit beside the notch, with an icon for each tool. Rest the pointer on one and a panel drops from under it, in one black shape with the notch.

  • Opens when the pointer rests on it, or only on a click.
  • On a display without a notch, such as an external one, a small glass capsule in the middle of the menu bar does the same job.
  • Give every display its own island, or keep it to the built-in one.
  • Full screen is left alone: the island hides until the pointer reaches the top of the screen.

In the menu bar

Thin level bars and fixed-width numbers, so nothing shifts between 3% and 100%. Click for the popover.

System

A card for every part of your Mac.

CPU, GPU, memory, power, disk and network, each with a live graph.

  • Graphs that follow the rate

    Each card keeps its last 150 samples: 15 seconds at the fastest refresh rate, 5 minutes at the default. Click a card to open its tab in Activity Monitor.

  • Color only when it matters

    Cards stay black, white and gray. A graph turns amber, orange and red as load rises, and charge going into the battery is green.

  • Both directions at once

    Disk, network and power draw two lines either side of a zero line: read and write, download and upload, what the Mac uses and what charges the battery.

  • Top apps

    CPU, memory, GPU, network and estimated energy per app. Helper processes fold into their app, and CPU is a share of the whole Mac, so the rows never add up to more than the total.

  • Temperatures and fans

    CPU, GPU, memory and battery temperatures, and a plain-words reason when the Mac warms up. A heat warning appears only when the heat is out of the ordinary.

  • Power that makes sense

    Charge and time left on battery. On the adapter, a bolt: green while it charges, plain once the battery is full.

Your servers, when you ask.

Add a Linux server or another Mac and it gets its own tab beside This Mac. Sysbar sends a small read-only script over your existing SSH setup.

Sample numbers. The real thing reads your Mac.

A remote Mac can show a Power card too. It needs passwordless sudo for powermetrics on that Mac.

  • CPU, memory, load, network, disk and NVIDIA GPU where available, plus the busiest processes.
  • Nothing to install on the server.
  • Uses your ssh keys or ssh-agent. Sysbar never asks for or stores a password, and never accepts an unknown host key on its own.
  • Keeps watching after you close the panel for as long as you choose, so the graphs are there when you come back.
  • A failed login isn't retried, so it won't trip fail2ban.
  • Add a device by hand, or pick one from the Macs with Remote Login on in your network and your machines in Tailscale.

Tools

Small tools that live in the island.

Sysbar began as a monitor. The island now holds a few small tools too, and the shelf is meant to grow. Each one is optional: Settings › Features turns it on or off.

Stash

Off until you turn it on

Park files, text and links while you work, in stashes you name.

4 items

Drop files on a stash

Try it — drag the file onto a stash or the notch, then drag an item out. Or press Return on the file to pick a stash.

  • Named, colored stashes such as Work or School. Main is always there.
  • Files are remembered by bookmark, not copied, so a file you move or rename in Finder is still found.
  • Drag files onto the notch and drop them on a tile to choose the stash. Drag them out again and they stay.

Clipboard history

Off until you turn it on

Everything you copied, one shortcut away.

No matches

Try it — type to search, pick a kind, or copy a password.

  • Text, links, rich text, images and files, newest first. Search them, or filter by kind.
  • A shortcut you choose opens a panel at the pointer: type to search, ⌘1 to ⌘5 to filter, Return to copy it back.
  • Copies that password managers mark as concealed are never kept. Ignore any app, choose how long to keep things, and it all stays on this Mac.

  • A panel names what is warming the Mac, such as graphics work or macOS slowing down to cool, beside CPU, GPU, memory and battery temperatures.
  • Manual control with one slider, from each fan's minimum to its maximum. Fans return to automatic when Sysbar quits, crashes or the Mac sleeps.
  • The heat warning shows only when the heat is out of the ordinary. Set it to Off, Early, Default or Late.

Keep awake

A button on the System page

Keep the Mac awake, even with the lid closed.

0:59:59 left

Try it — pick a duration. Sample numbers.

  • One menu: 1 hour, 4 hours, until a time you set, until you turn it off, while the charger is connected, or while an app you pick stays open.
  • It ends by itself when the time is up or the battery runs low, and says why.
  • Optional: move the mouse when you're idle, so chat apps don't show you as away. Nothing is clicked or typed, and it asks for the Accessibility permission only when you turn it on.

Fans and Keep awake use a small helper that macOS asks you to approve once. It can only set fan speeds and keep the Mac awake. On a Mac without a lid, Keep awake needs no helper.

      Privacy

      Private and light.

      Sysbar reads your Mac, keeps what it keeps on your Mac, and stays out of the way.

      Local

      Stashes and clipboard history are stored on this Mac, in folders only your account can read. Copies that password managers mark as concealed are never kept.

      Quiet on the network

      No accounts, no analytics. Besides the SSH sessions you set up, Sysbar's only request is a daily check for a new release on GitHub, which you can turn off. Nothing about your Mac is sent.

      0analytics

      Signed and notarized

      Sysbar is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple. An update installs only if it is signed by the same developer.

      Light

      Under 1% of one core and about 20 MB of memory while it waits in the menu bar or beside the notch. Once the panel has been opened it settles at around 35 MB. It stops sampling while the Mac or its screens sleep.

      <1%of one core, at rest
      ~20 MBof memory, at rest

      Accessible

      Every control is named for VoiceOver, nothing relies on color alone, and Reduce Motion is respected. The aim is WCAG 2.2 level AA.

      0.6.1 Nov 27, 2026

      8 changes

      Download for macOS

      Requirements

      macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer). Intel Macs aren't supported.

      First run

      Sysbar starts in the menu bar. Settings › General moves it around the notch and turns on Launch at login.

      Updates

      Sysbar checks GitHub once a day and offers new releases in the app. It installs only ones signed by the same developer.

      Is Sysbar free?

      Yes. Sysbar is free and open source under the MIT license, with no accounts, ads or in-app purchases.

      Does it work on Intel Macs?

      No. Sysbar needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later on a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer).

      Does Sysbar send any data?

      No analytics and no accounts. Its only request is a daily check for a new release on GitHub, which you can turn off, besides the SSH sessions you set up yourself.

      Can it watch my servers?

      Yes. Add a Linux server or another Mac over SSH, with your own keys or ssh-agent, and see its CPU, memory, load, network, disk and NVIDIA GPU. Nothing is installed on the server.

      Build from source 
      git clone https://github.com/atanur/sysbar.gitcd sysbar && ./scripts/bundle.shopen build/Sysbar.app
      Why not the App Store?

      Sysbar reads power data through a system interface the App Store doesn't allow.
